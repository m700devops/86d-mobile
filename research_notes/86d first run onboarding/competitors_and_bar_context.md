# Competitor onboarding in US bar-inventory software, and the people who do bar inventory (2024–2026)

_Retrieved 2026-10-19/20. App Store pages, vendor sites, help centers and some trade press were read directly. Capterra, G2, Software Advice and Toast's blog returned HTTP 403, and Reddit could not be reached (the search API refuses reddit.com), so content from those sites appears only as search-result snippets and is flagged "(snippet)". ## 1. How competing US bar-inventory apps take a new bar from sign-up to its first count

### Takeaway
There are two onboarding models. Demo-gated, human-led setup is used by WISK, BinWise, Craftable, BirchStreet/AccuBar, BarOptix and Sculpture Hospitality; BevSpot also wraps its free trial in "white glove" onboarding. Self-serve sign-up is offered by Backbar (free tier), Lixor (30-day trial, no card), Bar-i Speed Count and Partender's app. Under either model, almost every product has the bar build a product catalog and storage areas before or during the first count, by spreadsheet import, invoice scan, barcode lookup or a vendor team. Most measure partial bottles by tapping a fill level or weighing them on a Bluetooth scale. Ratings counts in this niche are small: Partender's 232 is the largest found.

### Inferences
- The one first-run step every competitor shares is building the bar's product list: importing a spreadsheet, scanning invoices, looking up barcodes, or having a vendor team do it. That step is also where the vendors' own help text admits the time goes (Backbar's "most time consuming"; WISK's catalog setup sold inside a $750+ implementation fee). An app that builds the list as a side effect of the first count (scan a bottle, it is identified, type the number) skips the step competitors treat as setup.
- The products that publish a single-venue price and a free start (Backbar free, Lixor $59 with 30 days and no card, Bar Patrol about $59) sit at or below the price of 86'd's paywall. WISK and BevSpot sell setup labor instead: a white-glove trial, or an implementation fee plus a demo. A self-serve app with no human onboarding has to earn on screen the confidence those vendors deliver by phone.
- Lixor is the closest new self-serve rival in posture. It is iOS, has a no-card trial and its first count is its onboarding, and its Sep–Oct 2026 release notes show it moved permission prompts to the moment of use and added a first-count walkthrough on the user's own bottle. That suggests the same design pressures 86'd faces.
- App Store social proof is thin across the category (most apps have under 100 ratings), and Partender, the most-rated, hasn't shipped an iOS update since January 2022. Ratings are unlikely to be what decides a prospect.

## 4. Who does bar inventory in an independent US bar: when, where, how long, with what, attitudes to tech, and what "easy to get started" means

### Takeaway
Inventory is usually counted by a manager (bar manager, GM or owner-operator), sometimes with one or two others. It is usually weekly: 52% in Backbar's 11,240-response poll; monthly is second at 26%. The count happens while the bar is closed, late after the last shift or before opening, in liquor rooms, storerooms and walk-ins where connectivity is unreliable. By hand, sources put a count at roughly 2–8 hours (4–6 is the figure vendors repeat); app users report 1–1.5 hours or less. Half of operators still count inventory by hand (7shifts 2025), and spreadsheets and paper are the usual starting point. Operators say they are comfortable with AI (87%, Toast 2026), but actual use is lower (26%, NRA 2026). When choosing systems they rank reliability and ease of use first and want tools that don't need "weeks of training".

### Cited Findings

#### Who counts
- A manager carries the count: "A manager takes a clipboard or a laptop into the storeroom." — [Backbar how-to](https://www.getbackbar.com/how-to-do-bar-inventory). Bartenders "might only have access to inventory counting", while managers handle reporting and purchasing — [Backbar FAQ](https://www.getbackbar.com/bar-inventory-faq)
- A San Francisco bar-manager posting (snippet): "complete monthly inventory count either with the GM or with a supervisor", and receive weekly inventory "under the supervision of the GM" — [CareerBuilder](https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/bar-manager-bottle-club-pub-san-francisco-ca--79cf045f-318f-45c0-99f2-3f97f666402a)

### Inferences
- The person in 86'd's first run is most likely a manager or owner-operator who has blocked out a few hours, often late at night after close or before opening. They will be standing in a storeroom or walk-in, may have no signal, and arrive with a clipboard or spreadsheet habit. A first run that asks for setup (areas, distributors, pars, product imports) before the first bottle spends the scarce part of that window. One that starts counting immediately, works offline and treats the count itself as the setup fits the situation better.
- Weekly cadence (52%) means a first count that goes well becomes a weekly habit within days. Monthly counters (26%) may not come back before a 15-day trial ends, so the first session may be their whole trial experience.
- Reported comfort with AI is high, but actual AI use and "back-of-house tech" plans are around a quarter to a half. Expect curiosity rather than fluency: plain explanations of what the camera does, and a fast visible win, will matter more than AI branding.
- Ease of use ranks just behind reliability when operators choose systems. For an app used in a dark storeroom on bad wifi, "reliable" means counts are never lost, which reviews show competitors failing at.
