# Camera-first "point it and AI identifies it" apps: onboarding, capture, permission and uncertainty conventions (benchmark for 86'd)

Scope note: 86'd's scan = framed viewfinder -> tap shutter -> server-side AI names the bottle (two models; disagreement shows "the second AI read X — check the label"; unreadable label asks for a retake) -> user types the count on a number pad. No fill-level reading. Research date 2026-10-19/20. Sources before 2023 are flagged [OLDER]. "Via search excerpt" means the page body could not be fetched directly and the claim comes from the search tool's quoted excerpt of that page — treat as lower confidence than a direct fetch.

## 1. First run of leading identify-by-camera apps (account? screens/taps before camera? tutorial? opens straight into camera?)

### Takeaway
The apps that feel instant (Apple Visual Intelligence, iOS Notes scanner, Google Lens in the Google app, Seek) ask for no account and put the camera one press or one-two taps away; apps that monetize or sync (Vivino, Adobe Scan, Cal AI, PictureThis, MyFitnessPal Meal Scan) put sign-up, a quiz and/or a paywall before or around the first scan. Exact current screen counts for most third-party apps could not be verified from public text sources.

### Inferences
- The bar users meet daily in OS-level tools (Visual Intelligence, Notes, Lens) is "press/tap once and the camera is live, no account". 86'd requires an account first; App Review 5.1.1(v) permits this when there are significant account-based features (per-bar book, orders, sync), so it is compliant, but it is slower than the camera-first benchmark. Category peers that monetize (Vivino, Adobe Scan, Cal AI, MFP) also gate on account/paywall, so 86'd is not an outlier among paid tools.
- Cal AI's 25+ card quiz shows long pre-camera onboarding can coexist with commercial success, but its purpose is personalization feeding a paywall — not teaching the scan. NN/g and the HIG both point to short, interactive, contextual teaching; 86'd's three-step start screen sits at the short end.
- None of the identify-apps documented here teaches a "human enters a number" step during onboarding; MyFitnessPal Meal Scan (confirm foods and portions) and Sortly-style Quick Quantity (Q2) are the nearest analogues to 86'd's AI-ID + manual count split.

## 2. Capture conventions and expectations (shutter vs auto vs live, framing, live hints, torch, haptics, waiting, repeat-scan loops)

### Takeaway
Three capture models coexist: deliberate shutter (Google Lens iOS, Notes manual, Visual Intelligence's press), auto-capture when the frame is stable (Notes Auto, Adobe Scan "hold steady"), and continuous live recognition (Seek's ID meter, VisionKit DataScanner, Scandit). For many items in a row, the reference pattern is a large, always-available trigger, a small preview, audio+haptic confirmation per scan, and no modal interruption between items. Waiting guidance: under ~1 s feels uninterrupted, 2–10 s needs a visible busy state, beyond ~10 s needs progress and a way out.

### Cited Findings
- VisionKit `DataScannerViewController` ("scans the camera live video for text, data in text, and machine-readable codes", iOS 16+): `isGuidanceEnabled` — "whether the scanner provides help to a person when selecting items"; `isHighlightingEnabled`; `qualityLevel`; `recognizesMultipleItems`; `isPinchToZoomEnabled`; `regionOfInterest` — "The area of the live video… that the data scanner searches for items"; `isAvailable` — whether "a person grants your app access to the camera"; NSCameraUsageDescription required — [Apple docs: DataScannerViewController](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/visionkit/datascannerviewcontroller).
- WWDC22 session 10025: "When you enable guidance, labels show at the top of the screen to help direct the user"; quality levels — "Fast will sacrifice resolution in favor of speed in scenarios where you expect large and easily-legible items", "Accurate will give you the best accuracy, even with small items like micro QR codes or tiny serial numbers", "I recommend starting with balanced"; with `recognizesMultipleItems` false, "the center-most item is recognized by default until the user taps elsewhere" — [WWDC22 "Capture machine-readable codes and text with VisionKit" transcript](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2022/10025/) ([Apple video](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10025)).
- Auto-capture with manual fallback: Notes scans automatically in Auto mode; manual = shutter or volume button, then corner adjustment and Keep Scan; multi-page loop "add additional scans" — [Apple Support 108963](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108963). Adobe Scan: hold steady and it shoots by itself; auto-capture can be disabled — [BetaNews 2017, OLDER](https://betanews.com/2017/06/01/turn-your-phone-or-tablet-into-a-mobile-scanner-with-adobe-scan).
- Live recognition with visible progress: Seek's ID meter climbs kingdom -> species while the camera is pointed; tap to save once at species — [Seek User Guide 2020, OLDER](https://static.inaturalist.org/wiki_page_attachments/SeekUserGuide2020.pdf) (via search excerpt).
- Risk of fully automatic capture in cluttered scenes: a Scandit user review says capture is "so fast that sometimes you can't 'aim,' and the engine scans the first available code", suggesting "a 'Scan' button in the camera view" — [G2 review of Scandit](https://g2.com/products/scandit-barcode-scanning/reviews/scandit-barcode-scanning-review-10774139).
- Framing overlay convention (low-authority third-party guide): dark overlay with a transparent cutout and corner brackets, a one-line instruction, a subtle scanning animation, a torch toggle, and immediate feedback (haptic or highlight) on a read — [vp0.com viewfinder guide](https://vp0.com/blogs/barcode-scanner-viewfinder-ui-mobile). Scandit SDK 4.4 [OLDER]: scanning "can be restricted to a sub-part of the video feed" — [Scandit 4.4 release](https://www.scandit.com/scandit-barcode-scanner-sdk-verison-4-4-released/).
- Torch: Scandit 6.21 added torch controls to the scanning UI — "Turn it on to get that difficult scan, then back off to continue without missing a beat" — [Scandit 6.21 release notes](https://www.scandit.com/blog/scandit-6-21-release-sparkscan). iOS 26 Notes scanner adds a flash option — [Apple Support 108963](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108963).
- Haptics (Apple HIG, change log May 7, 2024): "Use haptics consistently… build a clear, causal relationship between each haptic and the action that causes it"; "Prefer using haptics to complement other feedback"; "Avoid overusing haptics… can become tiresome when it plays frequently"; "Make haptics optional"; standard notification haptics: Success "a task or action has completed", Warning, Error — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics) (via [JSON](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics.json)).
- Repeat-scan loop reference — Scandit SparkScan (docs v8.6.1, iOS): built for "scan-intensive workflows, such as inventory management in retail, or goods receiving in logistics"; trigger = "A large-sized, semi-transparent floating button that users can drag to position it in the most ergonomic position" (position remembered); mini preview "helps with aiming and shows scan feedback"; default audio + haptic confirmation; tap to scan, press-and-hold for continuous ("The scanner will stop when the trigger button is released") — [Scandit SparkScan intro](https://docs.scandit.com/sdks/ios/sparkscan/intro/). - Observed result times in this category: Cal AI claims "under 15 seconds" ([RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/cal-ai-product-teardown)); a competitor test measured 3.1 s average ([Nutrola](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/we-photographed-100-meals-and-tested-every-ai-food-scanner)); Delectable 2.6 s average in 2014 [OLDER] ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/eat/drink/2014/06/21/delectable-all-the-notes-the-wine-lover-will-need-in-an-app/691581)).

### Inferences
- 86'd's deliberate shutter matches Google Lens on iOS and the Notes manual path, and is the safer choice on a crowded back bar: VisionKit defaults to the centre-most item and a Scandit user complained auto-capture grabs "the first available code". 86'd's centre crop with the dimmed outside area (repo CLAUDE.md) is the same idea as VisionKit's `regionOfInterest` and the dark-overlay-with-cutout convention.
- For counting a whole shelf, SparkScan is the clearest benchmark: the camera stays ready, the trigger never moves, each item is confirmed by sound/haptic, and errors pause only briefly. A modal number pad after every shot is a heavier loop than SparkScan's tap-tap-tap, but it fits Sortly's "scan, then set quantity" Quick Quantity pattern. The quantity step is the inherent extra cost of counting (as opposed to just identifying).
- A two-model server read most likely lands in NN/g's 1–10 s band (an inference; measure it). HIG and NN/g then call for showing something immediately (e.g. the frozen captured frame) plus a busy state, and for anything that can approach 10 s, a visible way to move on (86'd's background re-identification sweep already makes "move on" possible). The NYU result is about chat answers and should not be read as "slower is fine" for a counting task, where speed is the point.
- Haptics: HIG wants one consistent haptic per cause; e.g. Success for "bottle named", Warning for "check the label" (disagreement), Error for "unreadable — retake". Not overused across a 100-bottle count, and optional.

### Gaps
- No public source documents what Vivino, Google Lens, PictureThis or Cal AI show while the photo is analyzed (frozen frame? progress percent?), or their live hints; this would need hands-on capture or video teardowns.
- The exact VisionKit guidance strings (e.g. whether it says "Slow down" / "Move closer") were not found in Apple docs or the WWDC22 transcript.
- No study found of acceptable wait time specifically for camera identification, or for repeated scans in an inventory loop.
- Scandit's general UX guidelines page (beyond SparkScan docs) was not found.

## 3. Uncertainty and correction (how apps show low-confidence/alternative results; PAIR and HAX guidance; partial automation)

### Takeaway
The consensus pattern for an uncertain identification is to show a short list of alternatives (N-best) or stop at a coarser answer, and to make correcting it a single cheap action; numeric confidence percentages are discouraged. PAIR and HAX both say to state up front what the AI does and how often it is wrong, explain the benefit rather than the technology, and keep a manual path. For split tasks (AI does part, human does the rest), PAIR's automation-vs-augmentation framing applies: automate the tedious part (naming the bottle) and leave the judgment/responsibility part (the count) with the person, with human oversight of the automated part.

### Cited Findings
- PAIR Guidebook, Explainability + Trust: confidence formats are categorical, N-best, numeric. Categorical: "Clearly indicate what action a user should take under each category of confidence." N-best: "Rather than providing an explicit indicator of confidence, the system can display the N-best alternative results"; "Showing multiple options prompts the user to rely on their own judgement"; example "This photo might be of New York, Tokyo, or Los Angeles." Numeric: "Numeric confidence indicators are risky because they presume your users have a good baseline understanding of probability." When: "If it doesn't make an impact on user decision making, consider not showing it"; "A misleadingly high confidence… may cause users to blindly accept a result." Trust: "Users shouldn't implicitly trust your AI system in all circumstances, but rather calibrate their trust correctly"; "Communicate the product's capabilities and limitations clearly to set expectations, and do this early." Plant examples: "This plant is most likely poison oak because it has XYZ features"; "This app uses color, leaf shape, and other factors to identify plants" — [PAIR: Explainability + Trust](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/) (page date not captured).
- PAIR, Mental Models: "Be up-front about what your product can and can't do the first time the user interacts with it"; "Introduce an AI-driven feature at the moment it is relevant to the user"; "People learn better when short, explicit information appears right when they need it"; lead with user value and mention the AI only when it shows how the experience improves; avoid promising "AI magic"; "make sure there's a default user experience that doesn't rely on AI"; on failure, "Let users know an error occurred and give them a way to complete the task manually" — [PAIR: Mental Models](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/mental-models/).
- PAIR, User Needs + Defining Success: "Automation is typically preferred when it allows people to avoid undesirable tasks entirely" (tasks that are "boring, repetitive, awkward, or dangerous"); "People often want to, or have to, remain in control when the stakes are high for their role"; "automation and augmentation work together to both simplify and improve the outcome"; "there should almost always be an option for human oversight" — [PAIR: User Needs](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/user-needs/).
- PAIR, Errors + Graceful Failure: "Explain why a certain result couldn't be given and provide alternative paths forward"; "When an AI system fails, often the easiest path forward is to let the user take over"; users need "awareness of the situation, what they need to do next, and how to do it"; error states should "tell the user what inputs the AI needs or how the AI works"; "Don't miss an opportunity to explain your AI system"; example of a plant classifier given a dog photo — [PAIR: Errors + Graceful Failure](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/errors-failing/).
- Microsoft Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction [OLDER, 2019; still the current HAX set] (Amershi et al., CHI 2019, Glasgow; 18 guidelines in four phases — Initially, During interaction, When wrong, Over time): G1 "Make clear what the system can do. Help the user understand what the AI system is capable of doing."; G2 "Make clear how well the system can do what it can do. Help the user understand how often the AI system may make mistakes."; G8 "Support efficient dismissal. Make it easy to dismiss or ignore undesired AI system services."; G9 "Support efficient correction. Make it easy to edit, refine, or recover when the AI system is wrong."; G10 "Scope services when in doubt. Engage in disambiguation or gracefully degrade the AI system's services when uncertain about a user's goals."; G11 "Make clear why the system did what it did. Enable the user to access an explanation of why the AI system behaved as it did." Example applications cited for G10: autocomplete that "usually provides 3-4 suggestions instead of directly auto completing"; a voice assistant that "let me know they had trouble hearing me". For G9, an example violation: an activity tracker with "no way to manually input or change the data" — [Amershi et al., CHI 2019 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf); [HAX Toolkit guidelines page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/).
- App patterns:
  - Seek degrades gracefully: the seven-dot ID meter stalls short of species when the app can't pin it down, and you can still take the picture; "it does not know everything, so it may be wrong" — [Seek User Guide 2020, OLDER](https://static.inaturalist.org/wiki_page_attachments/SeekUserGuide2020.pdf) (via search excerpt).

  - PictureThis: "each search provides several possible options", with a "no match" option if the plant isn't listed — [Cottage Life](https://cottagelife.com/outdoors/thorny-issues-solved-with-a-snap-and-an-app/); changing a result "works only if you know the name of the plant" — [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/?p=167352) (possibly dated). ### Inferences
- 86'd's disagreement flag ("the second AI read X — check the label") is a two-item N-best plus a categorical "check" state. That fits PAIR (show alternatives, say what to do) and HAX G10 (disambiguate when in doubt), and it avoids numeric confidence, which PAIR calls risky. Where it lags the convention is HAX G9: in the peers (Vivino's list of close alternatives, PictureThis's options), the fix is one tap on the alternative, whereas 86'd's documented fix is "remove the row and add the right one". A one-tap "use X instead" would match G9 and the N-best pattern. This is a design inference, not tested.
- The "unreadable -> move closer and retake" path matches PAIR's "tell the user what inputs the AI needs" and ML Kit's "ask the user to recapture" (Q5). Being specific about the cause ("too dark", "too far", "glare") would follow PAIR's "as specifically as possible", if the server can tell those apart.
- 86'd's start screen copy ("Point, tap, type a count. AI identifies each bottle instantly." plus the three tips; see Q4 for the source) already does what HAX G1 and PAIR's Mental Models ask: it says what the AI does and what the person does, in benefit terms. Two gaps against the same guidance: nothing sets expectations of how well it works (HAX G2 — e.g. that it sometimes asks you to check a label or retake), and "instantly" is a speed/capability promise PAIR's "avoid AI magic" warns about; keep it only if measured time-to-name on bar wifi supports it. Inference.
- AI names the bottle, human enters the count: this is PAIR's automation-plus-augmentation split. Naming from a label is the "boring, repetitive" part; the count carries responsibility (orders depend on it). HAX G1/G2 suggest onboarding should say plainly what 86'd does ("86'd names the bottle; you tap in how many") and roughly how reliable the naming is, which also matches 86'd's own rule not to claim fill-level reading.
- Seek's coarser-answer pattern has an analogue 86'd could consider, e.g. "Tito's — size not read" when the brand is clear and the variant isn't, rather than a full retake. Inference only.

### Gaps
- No official help text found for how Google Lens or Vivino present alternatives today (2025–2026); Vivino evidence is an older review.
- No quantitative study found on which uncertainty display (N-best vs categorical) works best for retail/bottle identification specifically.
- PAIR chapter pages showed no last-updated date in the fetch.

## 4. Camera permission (priming evidence, HIG pre-alert rules, App Review 5.1.1, purpose strings, after denial, iOS 17/18/26 changes)

### Takeaway
Apple explicitly allows a custom screen before the camera alert, but it must have exactly one button that leads to the system alert, titled like "Continue" or "Next" (not "Allow"), with no close/cancel or other exit; App Review has rejected a "Not Now" button on such a screen as recently as March 2026. Purpose strings must be one specific, active sentence saying how the camera is used. Evidence that priming lifts opt-in is consistent but weak for camera specifically (an older academic study on explanations plus vendor figures). Once denied, iOS never re-prompts, so a Settings route is required. No Apple change to the camera permission model in iOS 17, 18 or 26 was found.

### Cited Findings
- Apple HIG Privacy (change log: June 21, 2023 consolidation; read via [JSON](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy.json)) — [Apple HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy):
  - Timing: "Request permission only when your app clearly needs access to the data or resource… Ideally, wait to request permission until people actually use an app feature that requires access"; "Avoid requesting permission at launch unless the data or resource is required for your app to function."
  - Purpose strings: "Write copy that clearly describes how your app uses the ability, data, or resource you're requesting… Aim for a brief, complete sentence that's straightforward, specific, and easy to understand. Use sentence case, avoid passive voice, and include a period at the end." Correct example: "The app records during the night to detect snoring sounds." Incorrect: "Microphone access is needed for a better experience." (vague, passive) and "Turn on microphone access." (imperative, no justification).
  - Pre-alert screens (explicitly covering "camera, microphone, location, contact, calendar, and tracking"): "If it's essential to provide additional details, you can display a custom screen or window before the system alert appears." "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert. People can feel manipulated when a custom screen or window also includes a button that doesn't open the alert… Another type of manipulation is using a term like "Allow" to title the custom screen's button… Use a term like "Continue" or "Next" to title the single button in your custom screen or window, clarifying that its action is to open the system alert." "Don't include additional actions in your custom screen or window, unless needed to obtain a legal consent. For example, don't provide a way for people to leave the screen or window without viewing the system alert — like offering an option to close or cancel."
  - Listed under the tracking-request subsection: "Never precede the system-provided alert with a custom screen or window that could confuse or mislead people… will lead to rejection by App Store review"; "Don't display a custom screen that mirrors the functionality of the system alert… don't create a button title that uses "Allow" or similar terms, because people don't allow anything in a pre-alert screen"; "Don't show an image of the standard alert and modify it in any way"; "Don't add a visual cue that draws people's attention to the system alert's Allow buttons"; "Don't offer incentives for granting the request."
  - The HIG Privacy page contains no guidance on what to do after a denial.
- Apple HIG Onboarding: "If your app or game needs access to private data or resources before it can function, consider integrating the permission request into your onboarding flow… making the request during your onboarding flow gives you the opportunity to show people why your app or game needs their permission and the benefits of granting it. Otherwise, present a permission request when people first access the specific function" — [Apple HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding).

### Inferences
- 86'd's design (a start screen that explains, then the system prompt; a Settings link once `canAskAgain` is false) is the Apple-sanctioned pattern, and the start screen's note ("We'll ask for camera access next — it's only used to identify bottles you scan.") is the kind of context the HIG wants. Checked against the HIG's pre-alert rules:
  - Start screen: a single primary action, "Start Scanning", is a feature verb, not "Allow" (meets the spirit of the "don't title it Allow" rule; the HIG's example wording is "Continue"/"Next"). But the header's back/menu button is a way to leave without seeing the system alert, which the HIG says not to offer on a pre-alert screen. Mitigating: this screen doubles as the scan tab's landing page (also "Continue Your Count"), and the repo notes the menu button exists because App.tsx hides the hamburger on the camera. Whether a reviewer reads it as a pre-alert screen is a judgment call — flag, don't assume.
  - Blocked screen, `canAskAgain` = true variant: "Enable Camera" + "Not now" is the same shape as the pre-prompt rejected under 5.1.1(iv) in March 2026 ("Continue" + "Not Now"; "The user should always proceed to the permission request after the message"), and "Enable…" is close in meaning to the "Allow" title the HIG warns against. It matters only if a user can see that variant before the system alert has ever been shown (e.g. in a transient state); worth verifying when it is reachable.
  - Blocked screen, `canAskAgain` = false variant ("Open Settings" + "Not now"): no system alert follows, so the pre-alert rules don't bite in the same way; Apple's docs and Expo's `canAskAgain` doc both point to sending the person to Settings, which is what it does.
- Two different camera purpose strings sit in app.json. The `cameraPermission` one ("Allow 86'd to access your camera to scan bottles.") starts with the word Apple warns against ("Allow") and is shorter on purpose; the `infoPlist` one is closer to Apple's "active sentence, specific use" model. Which one ships depends on Expo config-plugin precedence; worth confirming in a built Info.plist (e.g. `npx expo prebuild` and inspect). Unverified.
- Evidence that a pre-prompt "materially lifts opt-in" (the repo's claim) is directionally supported (Tan et al. 2014; Apple's own "improves the chances that they grant access") but there is no rigorous camera-specific number; vendor percentages should not be quoted as facts. For a camera-only app the bigger lever is probably context: people who just tapped "Start counting" understand why the camera is needed.
- Because a denial is permanent until Settings (Apple docs), the Settings route plus re-checking on foreground (which 86'd does) is the necessary complement. App Review 5.1.1(iv) also asks for "alternative solutions for users who don't grant consent", and 86'd's manual add / barcode-less entry could be offered on the blocked screen. Inference.

## 5. Scanning conditions relevant to bars (dim light, glare, curved labels, look-alike variants, high shelves)

### Takeaway
SDK and app guidance converges on: enough pixels on the target (move closer / higher resolution / let the target fill the frame), sharp focus, a torch toggle inside the scanning UI for low light, shooting straight-on to cut glare, recognizing only the centre/selected item when several are in view, and a quick "recapture" or manual fallback when the read is poor. Look-alike variants (vintages, sizes, flavours) are the hardest case and are handled by letting the user pick among close alternatives.

### Cited Findings
- Google ML Kit (barcodes): input "must contain barcodes that are represented by sufficient pixel data"; "the smallest meaningful unit of the barcode should be at least 2 pixels wide"; "Poor image focus can impact scanning accuracy" — ask the user to recapture; prefer 1280x720 or 1920x1080, and lower resolution only if "the barcode make up the majority of the input image"; across video frames wait for "a consecutive series of the same value" before trusting a result — [ML Kit barcode scanning (iOS)](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/barcode-scanning/ios).
- Google ML Kit (text recognition v2): "Ideally, each character should be at least 16x16 pixels"; "There is generally no accuracy benefit for characters to be larger than 24x24 pixels"; "Poor image focus can affect text recognition accuracy… try asking the user to recapture the image"; for speed, make the text fill as much of the frame as possible — [ML Kit text recognition v2 (iOS)](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/v2/ios). (The page says nothing about lighting.)
- VisionKit: "Accurate" quality level for "small items like micro QR codes or tiny serial numbers"; centre-most item recognized by default when several are present; optional pinch-to-zoom; `regionOfInterest` limits where it looks — [WWDC22 10025 transcript](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2022/10025/); [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/visionkit/datascannerviewcontroller).
- Low light: Scandit added torch controls to the scanning UI for codes that are "difficult to reach or read" — [Scandit 6.21](https://www.scandit.com/blog/scandit-6-21-release-sparkscan); iOS 26 Notes scanner offers flash — [Apple Support 108963](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108963); third-party viewfinder guide recommends a torch toggle — [vp0.com](https://vp0.com/blogs/barcode-scanner-viewfinder-ui-mobile).
- Glare/reflections: Scandit 4.4 [OLDER] listed better recognition under glare and reflections — [Scandit 4.4](https://www.scandit.com/scandit-barcode-scanner-sdk-verison-4-4-released/). A wine-inventory vendor: good lighting and flat labels "dramatically boost first-try matches"; poor angles and glare cause misidentification; faded/damaged labels need manual entry — [InVintory: photographing wine labels](https://invintory.com/blog/best-practices-for-photographing-wine-labels-for-app-accuracy/); [InVintory: 12 fixes](https://invintory.com/blog/why-wont-my-wine-label-scan-12-fixes/) (vendor blog). A low-authority blog suggests a haptic pulse plus an "angle the device" message when low contrast is detected — [vp0.com](https://vp0.com/blogs/barcode-scanner-viewfinder-ui-mobile).
- Crowded scenes / neighbours: Seek — "Focus on one species in the frame"; try a different angle ("if you are looking at a leaf, try looking at a flower") — [Seek User Guide 2020, OLDER](https://static.inaturalist.org/wiki_page_attachments/SeekUserGuide2020.pdf) (via search excerpt). SparkScan Target Mode for "selecting among many close barcodes" — [Scandit SparkScan advanced](https://docs.scandit.com/sdks/ios/sparkscan/advanced/). Auto-capture grabbing "the first available code" — [G2 review](https://g2.com/products/scandit-barcode-scanning/reviews/scandit-barcode-scanning-review-10774139).
- Angle/distance tips from the MyFitnessPal Meal Scan vendor: scan items individually; "slightly moving the camera or changing the view angle can improve recognition" — [Passio](https://www.passio.ai/blog/essential-guide-to-myfitnesspal-meal-scan).
- Look-alike variants: Vivino's hardest aspect is vintage; corrections were mostly picking from "a list of close alternatives" — [JancisRobinson.com](https://jancisrobinson.com/articles/best-wine-labelscanning-apps) (OLDER, via search excerpt). Untappd's scanner mainly recognizes large, well-known brands — [ID.nl](https://id.nl/huis-en-entertainment/mobiel/smartphone-en-tablet/untappd-handleiding-zo-werkt-het-2).
- Blur as the top failure cause in receipt scanning, with manual entry as fallback — [Expensify help](https://help.expensify.com/articles/expensify-classic/expenses/Troubleshoot-SmartScan-Issues) (via search excerpt).
- Gloves/hands busy: hardware-button triggering "useful if your users typically wear gloves" — [Scandit SparkScan advanced](https://docs.scandit.com/sdks/ios/sparkscan/advanced/).

### Inferences
- A dim back bar is the ML Kit/VisionKit worst case (small text, low light, reflective curved glass). The conventions that transfer: an in-viewfinder torch toggle (Scandit, iOS 26 Notes), "move closer" guidance (86'd already does this on an unreadable label), straight-on framing to reduce glare, and recognising only the centre item (86'd's crop does this).
- Bottles on high shelves: ML Kit's "target should fill most of the frame" and VisionKit's pinch-to-zoom both imply that a zoom control or a "take it down / step closer" hint is the expected answer; a wide-angle shot from below is the hard case for label text. Inference.
